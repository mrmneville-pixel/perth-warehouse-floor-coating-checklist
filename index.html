Choosing a Warehouse Floor Coating: A Perth Checklist

For warehouses, workshops and commercial sites

Picking a coating for a warehouse or workshop floor comes down to how the floor is used. Work through these questions before you ask for quotes, and the quotes you get back will be far easier to compare.

The cheapest quote is often the thinnest coating. Ask every installer for the system name, the number of coats and the finished thickness.

What will the floor carry?

  • Foot traffic only, pallet jacks, or forklifts.
  • Steel wheels or dragged pallets, which wear a thin coating quickly.
  • Heavy racking, which needs the slab checked before anything goes on top.

What will be spilled on it?

  • Oils, fuels and coolants in a mechanical workshop.
  • Acids, caustics or cleaning chemicals in processing areas.
  • Hot water or steam wash-down, which rules out some standard epoxies.

Common systems, in plain terms

  • Roll-coat epoxy: thin, economical, suits light traffic and storage.
  • High-build or self-levelling epoxy: thicker, smoother, handles forklifts and frequent cleaning.
  • Polyurethane cement: for heat, wash-down and food or beverage areas.
  • Polyaspartic: cures fast, useful when the shutdown window is short.

Safety and layout

  • Ask what slip rating the finished floor will have, wet and dry.
  • Decide on line marking, walkways and forklift lanes before the final coat.
  • Outdoor or sunlit areas need a UV-stable top coat, as plain epoxy yellows.

Questions to put to every installer

  • How will the concrete be prepared: grinding or shot blasting?
  • Will you test the slab for moisture first?
  • How long until foot traffic, and how long until forklifts?
  • What does the warranty cover, and what voids it?
